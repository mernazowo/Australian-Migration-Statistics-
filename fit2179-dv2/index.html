Chapter 1

Forty Years of Opening Doors

Migration levels have changed significantly over the past four decades. Economic growth, labour shortages, humanitarian commitments and family reunification have all influenced who comes to Australia and how many people are admitted each year. Two patterns stand out: a long period of growth through the 2000s, followed by the unprecedented disruption caused by COVID-19 and the rapid recovery that followed.

Key insight: In 2020–21, migration fell to just 160,052 — the lowest since 2001. By 2022–23 it surged to 195,004, a record high, as Australia caught up on a backlog of applications.

Chapter 2

Who Migrates to Australia?

Australia's migration program is split into three streams: skilled migrants who fill workforce gaps, family members joining loved ones already here, and humanitarian entrants — refugees and asylum seekers. The relative importance of each stream has changed over time as Australia's economic and humanitarian priorities have evolved.

Where Do Migrants Come From?

Skilled migrants come from many countries, but a small number consistently account for a large share of Australia's skilled visa intake. India has become Australia's largest source of skilled migrants, while China, the United Kingdom, the Philippines and Nepal also contribute significant numbers.

Chapter 5

The Great COVID Collapse — and Recovery

Net Overseas Migration (NOM) measures the true population impact of migration — arrivals minus departures. The COVID-19 pandemic caused the most dramatic reversal in Australia's migration history. International arrivals fell sharply as border restrictions limited travel and migration. When borders reopened, arrivals recovered rapidly and reached record levels.

Net Overseas Migration: Arrivals vs. Departures, 2015–2024
Figures in thousands of persons. The gap between arrivals and departures is Australia's net population gain from migration.
The 2023 surge: NOM arrivals hit 739,000 in 2023 — the highest ever recorded. International students alone accounted for 267,100 arrivals, more than double any pre-COVID year.
Chapter 6

Do Migrants Take Jobs — or Create Them?

A common concern is whether migrants displace Australian workers. Labour force data provides a broader picture of how migrants contribute to employment growth. Between June 2020 and June 2025, Australia added 2.33 million jobs. Recent migrants — those who arrived in that period — accounted for 34.6% of all job growth, despite being a small share of the population.

Conclusion

Australia's migration story is one of adaptation and growth. Over the past four decades, migration has responded to economic needs, humanitarian challenges and global events. While migration levels have fluctuated, migrants continue to play an important role in population growth, workforce development and the ongoing evolution of Australian society.
